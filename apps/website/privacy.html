Privacy notice

Privacy

What WebRA stores when you play and when you have an account, why, for how long, and what you can do about it. WebRA is a free fan project without ads, trackers or analytics cookies.

Who runs WebRA

WebRA is run by its maintainer on Cloudflare (Workers, Durable Objects and D1 in Cloudflare's network). Questions and requests: open an issue or contact the repository owner on GitHub.

What stays in your browser

  • The game needs an account while the site says so (the admins can open it to everyone). Your settings, saves, replays, installed original content and texture packs stay in your browser (local storage and IndexedDB). Nothing of that is uploaded unless you sign in and sync your settings.
  • Rate limits (for example for map uploads, the content download and sign-in attempts) count requests per network address. The address is stored only as a keyed hash (HMAC with a secret key, so it cannot be turned back into the address), never as such, and counters are deleted after two days.
  • Maps you publish (the map editor's Publish) are public: their name, author field and content are shown to everyone.
  • A map you share in an online lobby is not published: it is stored on the server unlisted and only the players and spectators of the lobbies you shared it with can download it (a public lobby's listing shows its name only). It is deleted when no game used it for 30 days. When your account is purged, a map you shared is deleted with it, unless a game used it within the last day (someone else shared the same map): then it stays without any link to your account and is deleted once no game used it for 30 days. Maps you install, including ones from a lobby, are kept in your browser's map library.
  • When you and the other players save an online game, the save file stays in your browsers. The server keeps a small record so the game can be resumed with the same players: when it was saved, the session name, a checksum of the game state and of its orders and settings, and which account played which side. It is deleted after 90 days, and with the account of any of its players. Your data export lists these records.

Your account

When you sign up, WebRA stores:

  • your username, display name, avatar color, email address (optional unless email confirmation is required; an address you have not confirmed is removed after 7 days), role, account state and flags;
  • how you sign in: a salted password hash (never the password), your passkeys' public keys and names, and your Discord account id and username if you link Discord (WebRA reads your Discord id, username and email, and never your messages or servers);
  • your sign-in sessions: a hash of the session id, a short device summary such as "Firefox on Linux", a keyed hash of the address, and when they were created and last used;
  • your synchronized game settings (the settings document);
  • when the account was created, approved and last used, and the maps you uploaded while signed in.

The session cookie (__Host-webra_session) is strictly necessary for signing in; it is HttpOnly, Secure and first party, and no other cookies are set, except a short-lived one while you sign in with Discord and the play pass (__Host-webra_play). While the game is only for accounts, the server checks your account when you load it and then sets the play pass: your account id, part of the session's hash and an expiry, signed by the server and valid for 10 minutes, so it does not look your account up for every game file. It is HttpOnly, Secure and first party, holds nothing else, and is removed when you sign out.

Network addresses and bans

To stop abuse, moderators and admins can ban accounts, and admins can ban network addresses and ranges. To make that possible WebRA keeps the last 20 network (IP) addresses each account used, with when each was first and last seen. Only admins can see them. An address that was not seen for 90 days is deleted automatically every day, and all of them are deleted when the account is deleted for good. While you are in an online game, the server also notes which game you are in and from which address, so a ban can remove you from it; that note is deleted when you leave and at the latest after one day.

How long data is kept

  • Sign-in sessions: until you sign out, at most 30 days, or 14 days without use.
  • Email links: 1 hour (password reset) or 24 hours (confirmation), and they work once.
  • IP history: 90 days after an address was last seen.
  • Unconfirmed email addresses: 7 days.
  • Failed sign-in counters: one day.
  • Maps shared in online lobbies (not published): 30 days after a game last used them.
  • Records of saved online games: 90 days (at most 20 per account).
  • Expired IP bans: 90 days after they end.
  • The admin audit log (what admins and moderators changed): 365 days.
  • Deleted accounts: kept for 30 days (so a mistaken deletion can be undone), then purged with everything stored about them. During those 30 days the account keeps its username and email address, so nobody else can take them yet, and admins can still see it. Audit log entries about a purged account keep only that an action happened.

Your rights

  • Download everything stored about your account as a JSON file from your account page (data export).
  • Correct your profile and settings yourself, or ask an admin.
  • Delete your account from your account page at any time.
  • Ask questions or object through the contact above.

Email

When email is enabled, confirmation and password reset emails are sent through Cloudflare Email Sending from noreply@webra.sherin.dev. WebRA sends no newsletters or marketing.

Original Red Alert files

The one-click install streams Electronic Arts' freeware Red Alert files from OpenRA's mirrors through WebRA's server to your browser. Nothing of it is stored on the server, and only accounts that are allowed to use it can.

The one-click music install works the same way: WebRA's server reads the soundtrack out of the freeware Red Alert CD images on the Internet Archive (archive.org) and streams it to your browser. The Internet Archive sees a request from WebRA's server, never your address, cookies or account. To keep downloads within a budget, WebRA counts how much music each account asked for per day, rounded up to whole megabytes, under a keyed hash of the account id, and the music requests per network address per hour, under a keyed hash of the address. Both counters are deleted after two days. If you install from a CD image yourself, the file is read in your browser and never uploaded.
