Privacy notice

Privacy

What WebRA stores when you play and when you have an account, why, for how long, and what you can do about it. WebRA is a free fan project without ads, trackers or analytics cookies.

Who runs WebRA

WebRA is run by its maintainer on Cloudflare (Workers, Durable Objects and D1 in Cloudflare's network). Questions and requests: open an issue or contact the repository owner on GitHub.

What stays in your browser

  • The game needs an account while the site says so (the admins can open it to everyone). Your settings, saves, installed original content and texture packs stay in your browser (local storage and IndexedDB). Of these, only your settings are uploaded, when you sign in (they sync to your account). Replays are uploaded while you are signed in (see Replays in your account).
  • Rate limits (for example for map uploads, the content download and sign-in attempts) count requests per network address. The address is stored only as a keyed hash (HMAC with a secret key, so it cannot be turned back into the address), never as such, and counters are deleted after two days.
  • Maps you publish (the map editor's Publish) are public: their name, author field and content are shown to everyone.
  • A map you share in an online lobby is not published: it is stored on the server unlisted and only the players and spectators of the lobbies you shared it with can download it (a public lobby's listing shows its name only). It is deleted when no game used it for 30 days. When your account is purged, a map you shared is deleted with it, unless a game used it within the last day (someone else shared the same map): then it stays without any link to your account and is deleted once no game used it for 30 days. Maps you install, including ones from a lobby, are kept in your browser's map library.
  • When you and the other players save an online game, the save file stays in your browsers. The server keeps a small record so the game can be resumed with the same players: when it was saved, the session name, a checksum of the game state and of its orders and settings, and which account played which side. It is deleted after 90 days, and with the account of any of its players. Your data export lists these records.

Your account

When you sign up, WebRA stores:

  • your username, display name, avatar color, email address (required for new accounts; it is never shown to other players, and an address you have not confirmed is never used for anything but the confirmation emails you ask for), when it was set, role, account state and flags;
  • how you sign in: a salted password hash (never the password), your passkeys' public keys and names, and your Discord account id and username if you link Discord (WebRA reads your Discord id, username and email, and never your messages or servers);
  • your sign-in sessions: a hash of the session id, a short device summary such as "Firefox on Linux", a keyed hash of the address, and when they were created and last used;
  • your synchronized game settings (the settings document);
  • when the account was created, approved and last used, and the maps you uploaded while signed in.

The session cookie (__Host-webra_session) is strictly necessary for signing in; it is HttpOnly, Secure and first party, and no other cookies are set, except a short-lived one while you sign in with Discord and the play pass (__Host-webra_play). While the game is only for accounts, the server checks your account when you load it and then sets the play pass: your account id, part of the session's hash and an expiry, signed by the server and valid for 10 minutes, so it does not look your account up for every game file. It is HttpOnly, Secure and first party, holds nothing else, and is removed when you sign out.

Replays in your account

While you are signed in and "Save replays to my account" is on (it is on unless you switch it off in the game menu's settings or on your account page), the game uploads the replay of every game you finish or leave: skirmish, online games, missions and co-op. A replay holds the game's settings, the map, every order given and, for online games, the names of the other players and the chat as you saw it. The server also keeps a summary for your list: when it was uploaded and played, the title, map, mode, the players' names, sides and teams, the game's length and result, the game version and the file's size and checksum.

  • Only you can list, watch, download or delete the replays in your account. Other players never see them, not even the ones they played in; admins can see neither the list nor the files through WebRA.
  • Each replay is deleted 30 days after its upload, automatically every day. Your account keeps at most 100 replays and 100 MB; when a new one does not fit, the oldest go first.
  • You can delete any of them at any time. Deleting your account deletes all of them at once (not after the 30 days other account data waits).
  • The files are stored with Cloudflare R2 (Cloudflare runs WebRA's server), compressed, under a random name. Your data export lists the replays; the files themselves download from the Replays list.
  • Until it is uploaded, a replay waits in this browser (IndexedDB), for the account that played it: when you are offline, or signed out because your session ended, it waits until that account signs in here again, at most 7 days (and at most 10 replays), then it is dropped. Signing out yourself, in the game or on the account page, drops your replays still waiting in this browser.
  • Switching the setting off stops new uploads (and drops replays still waiting to be uploaded in this browser); replays already in your account stay until they expire or you delete them.

Network addresses and bans

To stop abuse, moderators and admins can ban accounts, and admins can ban network addresses and ranges. To make that possible WebRA keeps the last 20 network (IP) addresses each account used, with when each was first and last seen. Only admins can see them. An address that was not seen for 90 days is deleted automatically every day, and all of them are deleted when the account is deleted for good. While you are in an online game, the server also notes which game you are in and from which address, so a ban can remove you from it; that note is deleted when you leave and at the latest after one day.

How long data is kept

  • Sign-in sessions: until you sign out, at most 30 days, or 14 days without use.
  • Email links: 1 hour (password reset) or 24 hours (confirmation), and they work once.
  • IP history: 90 days after an address was last seen.
  • Accounts waiting for their email confirmation: deleted 7 days after the address was set if it is still not confirmed (the address is removed at once, the rest after 30 days as for any deleted account). Other unconfirmed addresses stay on their account until you change them or delete the account.
  • Failed sign-in counters: one day.
  • Maps shared in online lobbies (not published): 30 days after a game last used them.
  • Records of saved online games: 90 days (at most 20 per account).
  • Replays in your account: 30 days after the upload (at most 100 replays and 100 MB per account), and at once when you delete them or your account.
  • Expired IP bans: 90 days after they end.
  • The admin audit log (what admins and moderators changed): 365 days.
  • Deleted accounts: kept for 30 days (so a mistaken deletion can be undone), then purged with everything stored about them. During those 30 days the account keeps its username, so nobody else can take it yet, and admins can still see it; its email address and Discord account are released at once, so you can sign up again with them. Audit log entries about a purged account keep only that an action happened.

Your rights

  • Download everything stored about your account as a JSON file from your account page (data export).
  • Correct your profile and settings yourself, or ask an admin.
  • Delete your account from your account page at any time.
  • Ask questions or object through the contact above.

Email

When email is enabled, WebRA sends only account emails: the link to confirm your address (when you sign up, change it or ask for a new one), a note to an address that already belongs to an account when someone tries to use it again, and password reset links. They are sent through Cloudflare Email Sending from a no-reply address of the site, are plain text with a simple HTML version, and contain no images or tracking. Cloudflare processes the address and the message to deliver it. WebRA sends no newsletters or marketing. While email is not enabled, your address is stored unconfirmed and nothing is sent.

Error reports

Error reporting is currently off: neither your browser nor WebRA's server sends error reports anywhere. If the site's operator turns it on, this section will say what a report holds and where it goes, and offer a switch to turn it off for your browser.

When something breaks in the game or on this website, your browser sends an error report to WebRA's own Sentry server at sentry.vitrion.dev, run by WebRA's maintainer (not Sentry the company). WebRA's server sends its own errors there too. Reports help find and fix bugs; nothing in them is used for anything else, and they are not shared.

A report holds:

  • the error: its type, message and stack trace (where in WebRA's code it happened);
  • WebRA's version and build, whether it came from the game, the map editor or the website, the game mode (menu, skirmish, mission, online, replay), whether WebGPU is available, whether original graphics are in use (on or off, never file names) and the multiplayer protocol version;
  • for errors of the game engine: the engine version, the game tick and the last few checksums of the game state (numbers that say whether two players' games still agree, not what is in them);
  • your browser and operating system with their versions (from the browser's user agent) and the page address, with the values after ? and # removed;
  • the last 50 steps before the error: which kind of button or element was clicked (never its text), pages opened, requests to WebRA's server (address without the values after ?, method and status) and the game's warnings and error messages.

Reports never hold your username, display name, email address, account id, chat, player, lobby or map names, cookies, sign-in or rejoin keys, or anything you typed: the game removes such text from messages before a report leaves your browser. Reports carry no network address; reports from your browser also tell Sentry not to take it from the connection, and the Sentry server is set not to store addresses. A small share of page loads (5%) also sends how long the page took to load, with the same limits. There are no session recordings. Reports are kept for 90 days.

Error reports follow the setting in the game (Settings, Privacy): on unless you switch them off.

The switch covers this browser: what your browser sends. The same switch is in the game's Settings under Privacy. When you are signed in it is part of your synchronized settings, so it follows you to other devices once the game or your account page runs there.

WebRA's server reports its own errors whatever the switch says, including a request of yours that failed on the server. Those reports hold the error, the address on WebRA's site that was asked for (without anything after ?) and the method, never your account, network address, cookies, headers or what you sent.

Original Red Alert files

The one-click install streams Electronic Arts' freeware Red Alert files from OpenRA's mirrors through WebRA's server to your browser. Nothing of it is stored on the server, and only accounts that are allowed to use it can. To keep downloads within a budget, WebRA counts the completed downloads of each account per day, under a keyed hash of the account id, and the downloads and download requests per network per hour (your address, or for IPv6 the first half of it that your network shares), under a keyed hash of that network. A download that does not complete is not counted. Admins can see these counts for an account and reset them. The game keeps the downloaded file in your browser so that installing again needs no new download; "Remove installed content" deletes it.

The one-click music install works the same way: WebRA's server reads the soundtrack out of the freeware Red Alert CD images on the Internet Archive (archive.org) and streams it to your browser. The Internet Archive sees a request from WebRA's server, never your address, cookies or account. To keep downloads within a budget, WebRA counts how much music each account asked for per day, rounded up to whole megabytes, under a keyed hash of the account id, and the music requests per network per hour, under a keyed hash of the network. The optional one-click install of the mission movies works the same way, with counters of its own (megabytes and requests per account per day, requests per network per hour). All these counters are deleted after two days. If you install from a CD image yourself, the file is read in your browser and never uploaded.
